## Planning a search
Define your target roles, locations, and must-have criteria before you start. Set a weekly routine, for example five tailored applications, three networking messages, and two hours of skill practice. Track every application in a simple spreadsheet with the company, role, date, contact, status, and next step.

## Where to look
Use several channels: company career pages, general job boards, professional networks, school career centers, and professional associations. Many roles are filled through referrals, so combine online applications with outreach to people at the company. For a location-based search such as software engineer jobs in a specific city, search by title and city, add remote or hybrid filters, and set alerts for new postings.

## Reading job postings
Separate required qualifications from preferred ones. You do not need to match every preferred item to apply. Note repeated keywords across several postings for the same role, because they show what employers in that field value most. Watch for red flags such as vague duties or unrealistic requirements.

## Keywords and applicant tracking systems
Many employers use applicant tracking systems that parse a résumé and let recruiters search by keyword. Use the exact skill names that appear in the posting where they are true for you, keep a simple single-column format, and avoid tables or text boxes for core content.

## Staying organized and resilient
Rejections and silence are normal. Review your results every two weeks: if you get no responses, revise the résumé or target roles; if you get interviews but no offers, practice interviewing.

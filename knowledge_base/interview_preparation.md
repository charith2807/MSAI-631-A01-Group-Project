## Behavioral interview questions
Behavioral questions ask about past situations, such as "Tell me about a time you handled a conflict" or "Describe a project that failed". Interviewers use them to predict future behavior. Prepare five to seven stories from work, school, or projects that you can adapt to leadership, teamwork, conflict, failure, deadlines, and initiative.

## The STAR method
Structure each answer with STAR: Situation (brief context), Task (your responsibility), Action (what you personally did, using "I" rather than "we"), and Result (the measurable outcome and what you learned). Keep an answer to about two minutes and spend most of the time on Action and Result.

## Before the interview
Research the company, its product, and the role. Re-read the job description and map each requirement to one of your stories. Prepare three to five thoughtful questions for the interviewer about the team, expectations in the first ninety days, and how success is measured. Test your video call setup in advance.

## Technical and case interviews
Practice out loud, not only in your head. Talk through your reasoning while solving a problem, ask clarifying questions, and state assumptions. If you get stuck, explain what you have tried and how you would proceed rather than going silent.

## After the interview
Send a short thank-you email within 24 hours that references something specific from the conversation. Note the questions you were asked so you can improve for the next round.

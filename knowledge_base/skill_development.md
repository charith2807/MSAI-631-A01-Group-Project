## Choosing which skills to build
Compare several job postings for your target role and list the skills that appear repeatedly. Rank them by frequency and by how much of a gap you have. Focus on one or two skills at a time rather than a long list.

## Learning plan
Set a specific goal, such as "build and deploy a small web app with a database in six weeks". Combine structured learning (a course or book) with hands-on practice. Schedule regular blocks of time, even thirty to sixty minutes a day, and review progress weekly.

## Projects as proof of skill
A small project shows more than a certificate. Choose a problem that matches the target job, keep the scope small enough to finish, publish the code with a clear README, and describe the result on your résumé. Document what you learned and what you would improve.

## Certifications and courses
Certifications can help when a field expects them, but employers usually value demonstrated ability. Prefer credentials that are named in job postings you want. Free resources, open-source contributions, and hackathons are valid ways to practice.

## Soft skills
Communication, collaboration, time management, and problem solving matter in nearly every role. Build them through team projects, presentations, mentoring, and volunteering, and be ready to describe examples.

## Honest representation
List only skills you can discuss and demonstrate in an interview. If you are still learning a skill, say so, for example "learning" or "coursework", rather than implying expertise.

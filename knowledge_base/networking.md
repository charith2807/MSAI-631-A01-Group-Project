## Why networking matters
Many positions are filled through referrals and personal connections. Networking is about building genuine relationships and learning, not about asking strangers for a job.

## Informational interviews
Ask for a short conversation of fifteen to twenty minutes to learn about someone's role and career path. Prepare specific questions about their day-to-day work, how they entered the field, and what skills matter most. End by asking whether they suggest anyone else you should speak with.

## Reaching out
Write a short, specific message: who you are, why you are contacting this person, a clear small request, and an easy way to say no. Mention a real connection such as the same school, a shared project, or an article they wrote. Follow up once after a week or so, and thank them afterwards.

## Using LinkedIn and events
Keep your profile consistent with your résumé, with a clear headline and a short summary. Join industry groups, attend meetups, career fairs, and alumni events, and engage with posts thoughtfully rather than only broadcasting. At events, prepare a thirty-second introduction and ask people about their work.

## Maintaining relationships
Share updates, congratulate people on milestones, and offer help when you can. Networking works best when you give as much as you receive.
